# Live demo script (6–8 minutes)

**Before the session** (5 minutes, once): `make lab`, open `http://127.0.0.1:8000/lab`, press
**Load demo case** and let every exhibit finish (≈ 2–4 min on a laptop). Leave it loaded: the
case and its analyses persist in `lab_data/`. Keep one or two of your own test files ready for
the "judge's file" moment, and a terminal for `make examine`.

Pitch line: *"Deepfakes and edited media are now evidence in Indian cyber cases — digital
arrests, voice-clone emergencies, forged notices. Investigators need to know three things
about every file: is it synthetic, was it altered, where did it come from — and they need an
answer that holds up in court. Satyapan Forensic Lab does that offline, on the examiner's
workstation, and shows its working."*

## 1. The case (30 s)

Open **Operation Pratibimb — FIR No. 0457/2026**. Read the one-line story: a retired
professor, a "digital arrest" video call, a notice, an officer ID, voice notes from his
"daughter" and a Hindi "CBI" note; ₹4,50,000 lost; the suspect's laptop seized.

Point at the exhibit grid: **Q** = questioned items, **R** = reference samples from the seized
laptop and from the family — "exactly how an FSL marks exhibits".

## 2. Intake and integrity (40 s)

Drop any file (e.g. one exhibit again → *duplicate detected by SHA-256*, or a fresh file).
Show the **live analysis stream**: integrity re-verified → 13–20 techniques ticking off.
Then open the **Chain of custody** tab: *"Every action is a hash-chained row. Change one byte
of this log and the chain breaks — verification is on the screen."*

## 3. The AI-generated "officer ID" (60 s)

Open `officer_id_photo_original.png` → **SYNTHETIC, very strong support**.
* Content Credentials: a *signed* manifest declaring `trainedAlgorithmicMedia` (generated by AI).
* Stable Diffusion's invisible watermark decoded bit-for-bit (chance of a false match < 10⁻⁹).
* The AI-image classifier independently says AI.

Then `IMG-20261021-WA0012.jpg` (the same photo as received on WhatsApp): credentials and
watermark are **gone** — re-compression strips them — yet the classifier still flags it, and
the **provenance graph** links it to the original as a near-duplicate. *"Signatures are
fragile; pixel forensics and cross-exhibit links are not."*

## 4. The forged notice (60 s)

Open `IMG-20261021-WA0011.jpg` → **MANIPULATED**.
* Copy-move: yellow lines join the cloned stamp to its source.
* ELA overlay glows on the pasted amount line.
* Graph → link to `notice_template_from_laptop.jpg`: the aligned **difference map boxes the
  addressee and the amount** — *"the forgery was made from the template found on the
  suspect's laptop: that is source attribution."*

## 5. The voices (75 s)

* `PTT-20261021-WA0009.opus` (Hindi): synthetic-voice windows on the score timeline, the
  **Hindi transcript with an English translation**, and the fraud lexicon highlighting
  "digital arrest", "safe account", OTP and secrecy.
* `PTT-20261021-WA0007.opus` ("daughter"): the graph shows it is the **same speaker** as the
  family's known sample `daughter_known_voice_sample.m4a`, and the voice analysis shows the
  synthesis evidence → *"a clone of her voice, not her."* Point out the joins of digital
  silence the splice detector found between sentences.

## 6. The deepfake video call (60 s)

Open `VID-20261021-WA0003.mp4`: play it; the **face timeline** shows the elevated windows; the
contact sheet and the most suspicious face crops. Then the graph: **same face** as
`profile_photo_source.jpg` and **same scene** as `laptop_seized_clip_0193.mp4` — *"the
deepfake was assembled from a stolen profile photo and a real clip on the suspect's laptop."*
Be candid: *"on these modern online face-swaps the face model alone says 'suspicious', not
'synthetic' — our validation page says so — the links make the case."*

## 7. The defence's call recording (45 s)

Open `call_recording_20261021_1412.wav` → **MANIPULATED**. Show the **ENF chart**: the 50 Hz
grid hum drifts smoothly, then jumps at 19 s — two recordings made on different days were
joined; the background-noise step at the same moment confirms it. *"This is the classic FSL
technique, automated."*

## 8. The report (45 s)

Reports tab → generate → open the PDF: cover with QR verification code, hash report,
methodology with references, one page per exhibit with figures, custody chain, **known error
rates**, and the **Section 63(4)(c) BSA 2023 certificate (Part B) pre-filled with the hashes —
for the examiner to verify and sign**. *"The software never signs or submits anything."*

## 9. The judge's own file (open-ended)

Invite a judge to drop any image, video or audio. Talk through whatever comes back,
including *"could not be checked"* items. If a technique abstains, say why (no face, too little
speech) — abstaining honestly is a feature.

## 10. Close (20 s)

*"Offline on an FSL workstation, 22 techniques, deterministic verdict rules, measured error
rates, court-ready reporting. Next: a three-month pilot with one cyber cell and one FSL,
validation on Indian casework, NABL scope and Section 79A notification."*
(→ `docs/forensics/DEPLOYMENT_ROADMAP.md`)

## Fallbacks

* Laptop without models: the same case still runs; affected techniques show *unavailable*.
* No network needed at any point.
* Terminal alternative: `make examine F=data/forensics/demo REPORT=1` prints every verdict,
  link and the custody-chain status, and writes the PDF.

## Q&A crib

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| How accurate is it? | Per technique, on held-out public data: VALIDATION.md (e.g. face video AUC 0.946, 0/563 real videos called fake; AI images AUC 0.88 with 2.5 % false alarms at the strict threshold; voice AUC 0.92 on real-world deepfakes). We list failure cases too. |
| Can an attacker evade it? | Yes, any single detector — which is why verdicts need several independent techniques, signatures and cross-exhibit links, and why "no indications" never means authentic. |
| Is this admissible? | It supports an expert opinion: hash-first intake, write-once storage, custody chain, method references, known error rates, ENFSI wording and the BSA s.63 Part B draft. The examiner gives the opinion and signs. |
| Why not cloud? | Evidence cannot leave the lab; everything runs on CPU locally. |
| What about Indian languages? | Whisper transcribes Hindi/Hinglish (and translates), the fraud lexicon covers English, Hinglish and Devanagari, and the voice head was trained with Hindi real speech so Hindi is not mistaken for synthetic. |
