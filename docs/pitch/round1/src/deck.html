The deepfake “boss scam” has reached India’s finance desks

It is happening in India, now
JULY 2026 · ₹10.70 CRORE · LISTED-COMPANY SUBSIDIARY [R1]
Starmangalsutra Pvt Ltd, a subsidiary of Sky Gold & Diamonds, disclosed that an employee sent funds to unknown accounts on instructions from an impersonated director. The company cited purported deepfake techniques and a compromised company phone and laptop. The director had given no such instruction.
17 JULY 2026 · SEBI CAUTION TO LISTED COMPANIES, PR 40/2026 [R2]
“Boss scam”: CEO/MD impersonation using AI deepfakes and voice cloning on video calls, hijacked WhatsApp Web sessions, the attacker’s number saved under the CEO’s name, and secrecy “because it is price-sensitive”. Then a mule account. SEBI’s advice: call the senior directly, never transfer on a message alone, report on 1930.
2024 · ARUP, HONG KONG · HK$200M (ABOUT US$25.6M) [R3]
An employee made 15 transfers after a video call with a deepfaked CFO and colleagues. The person who should have verified was the person being deceived.
Scale: Indian citizens lost ₹22,845.73 crore to cyber fraud in 2024 (Lok Sabha reply, 22 Jul 2025; mostly consumer fraud) [R4]. Corporate losses come on top, and most are never disclosed.
Primary user
Priya, accounts-payable maker at a ₹80-crore Pune manufacturer (illustrative). Pays vendors by NEFT / RTGS / UPI. Checker: Arjun. CFO: Rajesh.
Harmful moment
Priya is about to pay a new or changed bank account, in a hurry and in secret, because “the CFO” or “the vendor” said so.
Exact threat pattern
Harm
Loss of lakhs to crores in minutes. Recovery depends on reporting the transaction reference (UTR) within hours. Listed firms must disclose; Sky Gold’s shares fell on the news [R1].
Sharply bounded problem
In scope: payment instructions reaching an SME or mid-market AP team by email, WhatsApp text or voice note, and browser meetings (Google Meet, Teams on the web).
Out of scope (MVP): consumer scams, phone-call interception, native Teams / Zoom desktop apps (upload the recording instead).

Safety rules, property-tested on the real engine
  • Untrusted content can only raise the band, never lower it.
  • The floor reads only trusted data (fuzzed with hostile inputs).
  • LLM or style signals: at most VERIFY, never “strong”.
  • No HOLD from one uncorroborated ask; only the checker clears HOLD.
  • No code path files a report; a human always submits.
Detectors decide who verifies, never whether money to a new or changed account needs verifying.
A deepfake works by manipulating the person who would verify, so any strong signal moves the decision to someone else.

Pitch deck 6 / 10 Core approach: the innovation Value and differentiation (15) · Architecture and detection (20)

Satyapan Seal: prove the real executive instead of chasing the fake

Why detection alone decays
  • On 2026 video generators the best detector reached 69.7% AUROC; one detector scored 34.5% on closed-source generators (below 50% = worse than chance) [R10].
  • Open-source detectors lost about half their AUC on real 2024 deepfakes; audio −48% [R11].
A seal does not decay: the attacker needs the key, not a better voice.
Not sealed ≠ fake
A missing seal only raises to VERIFY. A seal never lowers a band or clears a HOLD (property-tested).
How it works · one Touch ID per meeting
1
Enrol once. Rajesh (CFO) creates a passkey on his laptop. The server keeps only the public key.
2
Start a meeting. One Touch ID signs a commitment to a fresh chain of keys, bound to this meeting ID.
3
Speak. His client adds a faint keyed level pattern (±2 dB, 12 bands) to every 5 s of his voice. Each key is revealed only after its 5 s end (TESLA delayed disclosure [R14]).
4
Verify. Priya’s panel checks each 5 s with the revealed key: “Rajesh: sealed ✓” or “Claims to be Rajesh: NOT sealed → verify”.
Intent Seal. For money, the same passkey signs SHA-256 of the exact amount + beneficiary + case + nonce + expiry. Any later change breaks it (page 5, step 4).

Pitch deck 8 / 10 Privacy, security, safety and misuse safeguards Safety, privacy and responsible AI (15) · Final check 05

Build for Trust: the brief’s five questions, answered

1 · What data do you need?
  • Payment entry, vendor master, payment register, directory: set the floor and the only callback numbers (company records).
  • Email, invoice, voice note the user chooses to attach: can only raise.
  • Remote tab audio, only while Priya turns protection on: asks + seal check; not stored unless she saves 30 s as evidence.
  • Executives’ passkey public keys and 32-byte seal keys: opt-in, own Touch ID.
We do not collect: voiceprints or any biometric, video, the user’s own mic, bank logins or OTPs, customer data, other tabs, contacts.
2 · What happens when the system is wrong?
  • False alarm: the worst case is “verify before paying”. No auto-block, no accusation; one uncorroborated ask → at most VERIFY. Routine payments at HOLD: 3.0% (synthetic, n = 200).
  • Missed attack: the floor still applies; a new or changed account always needs verification.
  • Real CFO not sealed (on his phone): friction, not harm → normal callback.
  • Correction: checker clears with a logged reason, can remove watchlist entries; monthly false-alarm review.
3 · Who can act on the result?
  • Maker: clears VERIFY by callback on the number on file or OOB-Confirm.
  • Checker: the only role that clears HOLD, after own callback + security sign-off.
  • Checker / security: mark confirmed fraud. A human files 1930 / NCRP / bank reports.
  • Urgent override: two senior approvers, reason, review in 48 h; never on suspected fraud.
  • Small firm where the owner is the target: CA or bank RM as second verifier (design).
4 · How will users understand the result?
Band + plain reason + next step, in English and Hindi for all 33 reason codes. Never a bare score.
“The bank account differs from the vendor master. Call the vendor back on the number already on file (not the one in the message) before paying.”
“बैंक खाता वेंडर मास्टर से अलग है। भुगतान से पहले वेंडर को पहले से दर्ज नंबर पर फिर से कॉल करें (संदेश वाले नंबर पर नहीं)।”
Seal text says “Not sealed does not mean fake”. An optional LLM summary must pass a faithfulness check and never sets the band.
5 · How will the product resist misuse?
  • Oracle tuning: makers never see scores; no public upload endpoint.
  • Prompt injection: the LLM has no tools, sees untrusted text only as data, cannot set or lower a band.
  • Adaptation: floor and Seal do not depend on detector quality; red-team numbers on page 7.
  • Evidence access: role-checked reads, hash-chained custody; in production S3 Object Lock + KMS, CloudTrail.
  • Privacy abuse: nobody can seal another person’s voice without that person’s passkey; keys expire.
Bias, consent and notice
  • Seal: its false-alarm statistics do not depend on voice, accent or language; English and Hindi voices measured alike.
  • Detectors: Devanagari recall is weakest (0.15). Before any voice threshold goes live: false alarms per speaker on consented Meet audio, ≥ 4 accents. “Inconclusive” never escalates alone; unsupported language → checker.
  • Notice: staff policy notice; execs opt in per meeting; meeting-chat notice to the other side (design).

Decision rules
  • Band = max(floor, every signal’s target); nothing lowers it. Strong signals from ≥ 2 groups ⇒ HOLD (style / LLM never count).
  • Policy 1.1: one ask alone ⇒ at most VERIFY unless corroborated (2nd ask type, another non-LLM group, or a risky floor).
  • “No, not me” ⇒ HOLD + suspected fraud. Grey flags (parse failure, detector error, short speech, unsupported language) ⇒ ≥ VERIFY + checker queue.
  • Clearing changes the status, never the band.

Residual risks, stated
  • A perfect deepfake on a known-vendor, same-account payment can pass, but the money then goes to the genuine vendor.

A voice-detector table (“EER 4% clean, 0% Opus…”) and fitted thresholds were produced by a script that drew random numbers instead of scoring audio.
